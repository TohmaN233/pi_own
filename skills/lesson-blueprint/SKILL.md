# Learning blueprint

Use this for a substantial lesson, unit or course plan. Answer a small factual question directly without forcing a planning ceremony. The active Host contract, allowed sources and teacher constraints remain authoritative.

## Plan from understanding and evidence

Plan backward from evidence of understanding. Start by naming the concept and the durable understanding that should transfer beyond today's example. Write one or two guiding questions that reveal the central tension, relationship or decision. These questions guide planning; they do not need to appear as framework labels in learner-facing material.

Identify the audience, time budget, required prerequisites, likely misconceptions and source boundaries. Separate prerequisites proved by completed work from prerequisites merely assumed by an earlier plan. If evidence of prior learning is unavailable, mark the assumption and include a quick diagnostic.

Choose evidence before activities. Useful evidence asks the learner to do something that could distinguish understanding from imitation:

- explain a mechanism or justify a step in their own words;
- predict an outcome and give a reason;
- diagnose an incorrect example;
- compare two cases and name the decisive difference;
- solve or explain a new case with changed surface details;
- state where an analogy, rule or model stops applying.

A recall question can check a prerequisite, but it cannot by itself prove transferable understanding. When a larger performance task is appropriate, specify its goal, audience or situation, constraints and observable success criteria. Do not invent a learner performance during planning.

## Build the learning sequence

For each objective, connect three things explicitly: the evidence that will show it, the learner action that produces that evidence, and the explanation or example that prepares the action. Schedule prerequisites before dependent ideas. Motivate a need or puzzle before formal terminology when that helps comprehension.

Use a sequence such as orientation → prior retrieval or prediction → focused explanation → worked contrast → learner attempt → feedback and retry → transfer or boundary case → concise close. Adapt the sequence to the task; do not add every phase mechanically. Allocate real minutes to attempts and feedback rather than filling the session with exposition.

Include examples that expose structure, not just easy arithmetic. Add a counterexample or boundary when it prevents overgeneralization. Let activities and assessments use the same objective while differing enough that the assessment does not merely repeat the demonstration.

## Quality gate

Before saving, check:

- every objective has observable evidence;
- every activity advances an objective;
- prerequisite assumptions are visible;
- examples, exercises and visuals use consistent notation and sources;
- the timing fits the available session;
- the final task tests transfer, relationship or boundary rather than copying;
- teacher planning labels have been removed from learner-facing prose.

In Course Builder, express this work through the existing semester and lesson fields, save a new draft revision and wait for teacher approval. In tutoring, scale the workflow to the learner's actual question.
