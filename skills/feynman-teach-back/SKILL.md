# Teach-back

Use this for an explicit explain-back, review or misconception diagnosis after the learner has encountered the concept. Do not force it onto every question or demand an explanation of unfamiliar material.

## Live diagnostic cycle

1. Bound one concept and a realistic audience. Ask the learner for an initial explanation without first supplying the canonical answer.
2. Read the learner's actual words. Separate a vocabulary problem from a conceptual one and identify only one or two gaps that affect the explanation's correctness or transfer.
3. Ask a short Socratic question, request an example, or give the smallest useful hint. Keep the learner's explanation as the working object rather than replacing it with a lecture.
4. Wait for explanation v2. Check whether the targeted gap changed and quote or paraphrase the learner's own improvement accurately.
5. Ask the learner to remove unnecessary jargon or define any term they still rely on. Do this from the actual explanation, not from an imagined version.
6. Ask for an analogy and one place where that analogy fails. A useful analogy must preserve the relevant relationship and expose its boundary.
7. Give a new case with different surface details. Ask the learner to apply the idea and explain which features matter.

If the learner remains stuck, provide a compact explanation tied to the identified gap, then invite another attempt. Do not withhold basic instruction merely to preserve a ritual.

## Evidence record

For a substantial cycle, retain: bounded concept, intended audience, explanation v1, diagnosed gaps, prompt or hint, explanation v2, analogy and boundary, transfer response and remaining uncertainty. Record only what the learner actually produced. The Host owns durable progress and completion state.

## Teacher preparation

Design the teach-back prompt, anticipated misconception signals, one or two diagnostic follow-ups, analogy boundary and transfer case. Put these in the lesson activity and checking fields. Do not invent learner responses, stage a dialogue among imaginary AI students or claim that a pre-generated page dynamically diagnosed free-form input.

The cycle is complete only when there is an actual revised explanation and transfer evidence, or when the remaining gap is honestly recorded.
