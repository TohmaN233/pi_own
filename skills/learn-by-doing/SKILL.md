# Learn by doing

Design each activity around a causal learning loop: what the learner does, what changes or becomes visible, what they should observe, and what conclusion the observation supports. Choose an action because it exposes the concept, not because an interactive format looks impressive.

## Activity contract

For each activity define:

- concept and misconception being tested;
- learner action or choice;
- variable, case or representation that changes;
- expected observable result;
- question that connects observation to explanation;
- feedback signal and retry path;
- transfer case or boundary, when useful.

Use prediction with a reason before revealing the result when the comparison can test that reason. Let the learner attempt the operation before feedback. Give a focused hint and another attempt rather than immediately completing the task.

Keep a scene or activity focused on one mechanism or comparison. Several controls are justified only when their joint effect is the object of study. Reuse an interaction pattern when it supports a new conceptual operation; do not enforce widget rotation or add characters and roles for cosmetic variety.

## Teacher preparation and live use

In a lesson plan, write the exact learner action, timing, expected observation and check for understanding. Prepare examples with controlled contrasts and an identifiable error signal. A planned activity is not evidence that learning occurred.

In live teaching, wait for the learner's action and response. Record what actually happened through the available Host. Do not simulate student responses or stage a dialogue among AI students as proof.

Use only interactions the current artifact implements. A static visualization can support prediction, reveal, comparison and annotation, but it cannot be described as a draggable or real-time simulation. State how the teacher will use it and what follow-up explanation to request.

The activity is ready when a reviewer can answer: What does the learner do? What do they observe? Why should that observation change their understanding? What can they try next?
